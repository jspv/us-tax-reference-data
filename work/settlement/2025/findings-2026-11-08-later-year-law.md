## 2. Georgia

Answer: YES, HB 463 ("Georgia Economic Growth and Tax Relief Act of 2026") was signed by Gov. Kemp on May 11, 2026 (governor press release dated 2026-05-11). Effective on approval, applicable to taxable years beginning on or after Jan 1, 2026. I found no act number on the press release.

Verbatim from the as-passed text (Sec. 2-1, 48-7-20(a.1)(1)):
> "On and after January 1, 2026, the tax imposed pursuant to subsection (a) of this Code section shall be 4.99 percent for taxable years beginning on or after January 1, 2026; provided, however, that such rate shall be reduced by 0.125 percent annually beginning on January 1, 2027, until the rate reaches 3.99 percent; provided that such annual reductions in the tax rate shall be delayed by one year for each year that prospective annual reductions in the standard deduction are delayed for any of the reasons provided in paragraph (1.1) of subsection (a) of Code Section 48-7-27."

Rate by year: 2026 = 4.99% (flat, retroactive). 2027 = 4.99% unless reduced; if no delay triggers, 2027 4.865%, 2028 4.74%, ... (0.125 steps) down to a 3.99% floor. Each step is delayed one year if, as of Dec 1, any of these is true: (A) Governor's revenue estimate for next FY is not at least 3% above current FY; (B) prior FY net revenue not higher than each of preceding three FYs; (C) Revenue Shortfall Reserve does not exceed the projected decrease from the next year's rate reduction. So 2027+ rates are contingent, determined each December 1 by OPB. The repo should model 4.99% for 2026 and either flat 4.99% or the scheduled path flagged as contingent. (Note the old law reduced by 0.10 to 4.99; HB 463 reduces by 0.125 to 3.99.)

Standard deduction (Sec. 2-3, 48-7-27(a)(1)(B)):
> "(i) In the case of a married couple filing a joint return, $30,000.00; provided, however, that such deduction shall be increased by $750.00 annually beginning on January 1, 2027, until such deduction reaches $36,000.00 ... (ii) In the case of a single taxpayer, head of household, or married taxpayer filing a separate return, $15,000.00; provided, however, that such deduction shall be increased by $375.00 annually beginning on January 1, 2027, until such deduction reaches $18,000.00, provided that such increases ... shall be subject to delays as provided in paragraph (1.1)"
- So: 2026 = 15,000 single/HOH/MFS and 30,000 MFJ (prior law 12,000 and 24,000). From 2027, +375 / +750 per year (cap 18,000 / 36,000, reached after 8 steps), each step delayed one year when any of the Dec 1 tests in (1.1) fails.
- Dependent exemption: $5,000 (was $4,000), +$125 annually from 2027 to $6,000, same delays (48-7-26(b)).
- Retirement exclusion: new 48-7-27(a)(5)(A)(xiv): "For taxable years beginning on or after January 1, 2027, retirement income from any source not to exceed an exclusion amount of $35,000.00 for each taxpayer meeting the eligibility requirement set forth in division (i) or (ii) ... or an amount of $70,000.00 for each taxpayer meeting the eligibility requirement set forth in division (iii)". Division (xiii) keeps $35,000 / $65,000 through taxable years ending on or before Dec 31, 2026. The 65+ cap is a fixed step ($65,000 to $70,000 in 2027), NOT subject to the revenue delay tests (no delay language in the text). Reading of the text; the question premise of a "fixed schedule" for the exclusion is right but it is a single step, not a schedule.
- Other: overtime exclusion up to $1,750 (2026-2028, repealed Dec 31, 2028) and cash tips up to $1,750 (same sunset).
- Addresses: https://www.legis.ga.gov/api/legislation/document/20252026/249080 (HB 463/AP PDF; bill id 70350 per EY, https://www.legis.ga.gov/legislation/70350); https://gov.georgia.gov/press-releases/2026-05-11/gov-kemp-signs-legislation-lowering-taxes-and-supporting-economic-growth
- Confidence: HIGH (statutory text read; signing confirmed by governor release). The GA rate path after 2026 is contingent; flag as such.

## 3. Colorado

Answer: NO change enacted for 2026+ to the pension/annuity caps, the Social Security subtraction, or the military subtraction, as far as I could find.

- SB25-136 "Expand Deduction For Retirement Benefits" (would have removed the 20,000/24,000 caps for tax year 2026): Lost. leg.colorado.gov status: 02/27/2025 Senate State, Veterans & Military Affairs Committee voted to postpone indefinitely (3-2). Not enacted.
- HB26-1062 "Expand Deduction for Retirement Benefits" (Rep. Weinberg; would remove all caps starting tax year 2027): postponed indefinitely by House Finance Committee on 02/09/2026 (PERA status page, updated June 2, 2026; fiscal note dated Feb 4, 2026 says "Starting in tax year 2027, the bill removes the cap on pension and annuity income deductions"). Not enacted. (An earlier search snippet implying a bill "signed March 12" was an error: it was the PERA page's list entry, and the page shows HB26-1062 as postponed indefinitely.)
- Current law per Colorado DOR (https://tax.colorado.gov/income-tax-subtractions): pension and annuity subtraction for age 55+, "limited to $24,000 for individuals age 65 or older" and "limited to $20,000 for all other individuals". Social Security: caps do not apply to Social Security benefits "received by individuals age 65 or older during tax years 2022 and later", and not to Social Security received in tax years 2025 and later by ages 55 to 64 with AGI at most $75,000 single / $95,000 joint. Military retirement subtraction (tax years 2019 through 2028): "retired military servicemembers under 55 years of age" and "The allowable subtraction is limited to $15,000."
- I did not search every 2026 session bill; no other pension/Social Security/military subtraction bill surfaced in the legislative council or PERA lists. Residual risk: a 2026 bill touching the Social Security age 55-64 AGI limits or the military subtraction that I did not find.

## 4. District of Columbia

Answer for tax year 2025: the repo's 15,000 single is correct per DC's own instructions, but it rests on a contested legal position. Federal 2025 single amount under P.L. 119-21 is 15,750.

- Normal rule: DC's standard deduction is tied to the federal basic amount via conformity (OTR FAQ says "now aligned" with federal; secondary report).
- DC action: the Income and Franchise Tax Conformity and Revision Emergency Amendment Act (retroactive to Jan 1, 2025) and Temporary Amendment Act of 2025, D.C. Act 26-217 (law without mayor's signature Dec 20, 2025; transmitted to Congress Dec 30, 2025), decoupled from P.L. 119-21 provisions including the higher standard deduction, keeping TCJA-level amounts. 2025 OTR D-40 booklet (Mar 2026 revision, file 2025_D40_Book_Final_wLinks_030526_v1.0.pdf; I could not retrieve it directly, secondary report via search): single/separate/dependent 15,000; HOH 22,500; joint 30,000; additional aged/blind 2,000 per the CFO fiscal impact statement.
- Congress: H.J.Res. 142 disapproved D.C. Act 26-217. Passed House Feb 4, 2026 (215-210), Senate Feb 12, 2026 (49-47), approved Feb 18, 2026 as Public Law 119-78 (govinfo: "disapproves of the action of the District of Columbia Council" on D.C. Act 26-217).
- DC AG opinion (Feb 24, 2026, via Bloomberg Tax and AFS Law, secondary): the resolution "does not alter District taxpayers' 2025 tax liabilities"; Congress missed the 30-day window; the Temporary Act "remains in effect" and "expires September 25" (2026), after which the code would again conform to federal law absent further legislation. Because OTR's 2025 forms still use 15,000 after the resolution, OTR evidently follows the AG. The 15,750 figure appears only in an unverified Reddit post. Open legal risk: if the disapproval is held effective, 2025 single would be 15,750 (federal OBBBA amount).
- 2026: the 2026 OTR D-40ES (April 2026, read directly from PDF text): "If you expect to take a standard deduction, enter $16,100 if single, married/registered domestic partners filing separately or a dependent. Enter $24,150 if head of household. Enter $32,200 if married/registered domestic partner filing jointly ... or qualifying widow(er)" and additional aged/blind "$1,650 (or $2,050 if the individual is also unmarried and not a surviving spouse)". These equal the federal 2026 amounts (OBBBA-inclusive), so for 2026 DC is treating the deduction as conforming to federal. Not a statute citation; no enacted DC law found that fixes 2026 separately.
